01
Principles

Four rules the rest follows from

Minor Index works inside the major stack and bends it. The identity does the same: it is built from found parts, made to survive bad copies, and kept local.

01

Degraded reproduction is the brief

Everything is designed to survive a photocopier, a thermal printer, e-ink and a low-resolution screen. That is not a limitation we tolerate; it is the constraint the identity is drawn from. If it only works on a good monitor, it is not finished.

02

Minor, not small

“Minor” is another logic, not a lesser one. The marks are rough, hand-made and singular on purpose. Do not clean them up into something that could belong to anyone.

03

Bricolage, not blueprint

The bricoleur assembles what already exists. The system has a fixed vocabulary — four marks, twelve bodies, five inks, one press — and new work recombines it. Nothing is drawn fresh in an editor.

04

A plate is code, and code is the original

Every image is reproducible from its numbers. If a plate is wrong, change the number that made it and print it again. Never retouch an export: the next export would lose it.

02
Marks

A family with no head

Four marks, drawn by Frank. None leads: each one is chosen by where it is going, and all four carry the name equally. They are distance fields, so each can be cut flat, screened, outlined, bitten or printed out of register by the same press.

Three cutsWhat Frank drew the figure two ways — filled with an inline, and as an outline — is a cut, and any mark can take it.

Clear spaceThe unit is x: the cap height of INDEX in the lockup, and one tenth of the box for the other marks. Nothing enters x on any side — type, furniture, the sheet's edge.

Smallest sizesBelow these the marks close up. Screened marks need room for the dots: at least twelve cells across the narrowest stroke.

03
Ink

Two plates, a marker, a stamp

Five inks, one table. Toner on the first plate at 15°, blu on the second at 75°, fluo laid flat like a highlighter, on white or grey stock. A sixth colour is a pass nobody paid for.

Screens, not tintsThere are no lighter versions of an ink. A lighter tone is the same ink at a lower dot area, and it shows its dots.

PairingsInk on ground. Struck-through pairs are not used: they either vanish on a photocopy or put fluo through a screen.

On pressThe hex values are the source. Everything else is a conversion.

CMYK values on this page are straight conversions for proofing, not separations. Blu and fluo are spot colours and are matched on press: blu to the Blu nCHANT 02 the film was printed with, fluo to a fluorescent yellow. Neither is a Pantone reference yet; record the swatch when one is chosen.

05
The press

Coverage becomes dot area

Every mark and body is screened the same way: a rotated lattice, toner at 15°, blu at 75°. The screen is set for the medium the work is going to, not for the monitor it is made on.

06
Bodies

Twelve bodies and an appetite

Beside the marks, the system grows bodies: the sun, the cell, the seed head, the spiral and their kin. They carry the identity where the name is not needed. What is done to them — twist, growth, bites, eclipse — is the appetite.

The appetiteDone to a field, in this order. Bites are taken at the contour, never at random.

07
Sheets

The formats plates are cut at

A plate is a sheet, not a canvas. These are the sizes, drawn to one scale; the furniture — trim marks, brackets, axes, a band at the foot — is placed by proportion, so it lands in the same place on every one.

FurnitureWhat a press leaves that is not the image. Use it to frame, never to decorate.
